Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in San Angelo

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

Our San Angelo team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • Rust at the connections above the tank, either the flex lines or the inlet and outlet fittings.
  • The relief valve for temperature and pressure opening, or failing, and letting water out.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Shared Garage Wall

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Heater Closet Drywall

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Adjacent Room Flooring

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

The wooden stand or subfloor under the tank takes the most water. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.
